Let \(x\in\mathbb R\) be arbitrary. Completing the square gives the exact identity

\[
\begin{aligned}
f(x)
&=x^2-6x+13\\
&=x^2-6x+9+4\\
&=(x-3)^2+4.
\end{aligned}
\]

Expanding verifies the identity directly:

\[
(x-3)^2+4=x^2-6x+9+4=x^2-6x+13.
\]

For every real \(x\), square nonnegativity gives

\[
(x-3)^2\ge 0.
\]

Therefore,

\[
f(x)=(x-3)^2+4\ge 4
\]

for every \(x\in\mathbb R\). Thus \(4\) is a global lower bound.

It is attained at \(x=3\), as direct substitution shows:

\[
f(3)=3^2-6(3)+13=9-18+13=4.
\]

The equality case is exhaustive. For any \(x\in\mathbb R\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
f(x)=4
&\iff (x-3)^2+4=4\\
&\iff (x-3)^2=0\\
&\iff x-3=0\\
&\iff x=3.
\end{aligned}
\]

Conversely, if \(x=3\), the direct computation above gives \(f(x)=4\). Hence

\[
f(x)=4 \iff x=3.
\]

Therefore, the **global minimum value is**

\[
\boxed{4},
\]

and the complete set of real points where it is attained is

\[
\boxed{\{3\}}.
\]
