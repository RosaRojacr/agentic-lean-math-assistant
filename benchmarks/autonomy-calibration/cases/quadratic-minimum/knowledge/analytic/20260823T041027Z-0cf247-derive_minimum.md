Let \(x\in\mathbb R\). Completing the square gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
f(x)
&=x^2-6x+13\\
&=x^2-6x+9+4\\
&=(x-3)^2+4.
\end{aligned}
\]

Expanding back verifies the identity exactly:

\[
(x-3)^2+4=x^2-6x+9+4=x^2-6x+13.
\]

Since \(x-3\in\mathbb R\), square nonnegativity implies

\[
(x-3)^2\ge 0.
\]

Therefore, for every real \(x\),

\[
f(x)=(x-3)^2+4\ge 4.
\]

Thus \(4\) is a global lower bound. Direct substitution shows that it is attained:

\[
f(3)=3^2-6(3)+13=9-18+13=4.
\]

The equality case is exhaustive. For every \(x\in\mathbb R\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
f(x)=4
&\iff (x-3)^2+4=4\\
&\iff (x-3)^2=0\\
&\iff x-3=0\\
&\iff x=3.
\end{aligned}
\]

In particular, the forward direction proves that \(f(x)=4\) forces \(x=3\). Conversely, if \(x=3\), the direct computation above proves \(f(x)=4\). Hence

\[
f(x)=4\iff x=3.
\]

Therefore the global minimum value is

\[
\boxed{4},
\]

and its complete attaining set is

\[
\boxed{\{3\}}.
\]
